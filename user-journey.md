# User Journey

Rebecca is scrolling through Instagram when she sees a coffee shop that looks fun to visit with her roommates. Normally, she might send the post to their group chat, where everyone reacts enthusiastically but the idea eventually gets buried under newer messages. Instead, Rebecca opens OnDeck. Her roommates already have a persistent group in the app. She opens it from the group cards and can invite someone new if needed(Sketches 1 and 2). She can invite someone by copying and sharing an invite link. Friends can sign up after receiving the invitation. She can rearrange the cards without changing anyone else’s layout.

Rebecca selects Add activity from the sidebar, enters the coffee shop’s name, and adds its link. She can also fill in cost, location, or a date, but none of those are needed to save the idea. She chooses Roommates and presses Save and share. Because Rebecca suggested the activity, OnDeck automatically records that she wants to do it. If Rebecca also wanted to add it to the SF Friends group chat she could.

Over the next few days, Rebecca’s roommates encounter the coffee shop while browsing their group’s On Deck (Sketch 6). Amy and Sarah open the activity and mark Want to do, while another roommate can indicate that they have already been there and whether they would do it again (Sketch 7). These responses do not require the group to make plans immediately; instead, OnDeck gradually preserves the context that would otherwise disappear in their group chat.

A few weeks later, Rebecca, Amy, and Sarah are together on a Saturday afternoon and want to do something, but nobody remembers all of the activities they had previously discussed. Rebecca opens their Roommates group and selects Pick for Now. She selects herself, Amy, and Sarah as the people currently making plans and optionally narrows the options based on constraints such as category, cost, location, or date (Sketch 8).

OnDeck resurfaces several activities that their group had previously saved, prioritizing options relevant to the people making plans (Sketch 9). The coffee shop appears near the top because all three had previously expressed interest in it. Rather than searching for activities from scratch or scrolling back through weeks of messages, they recover an idea they were already excited about and decide to go. OnDeck helps them preserve and reuse the group’s earlier enthusiasm without turning the final decision into a formal scheduling or voting process.
